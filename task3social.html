REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES

The Twitter Bitcoin Scam (2020)

Hackers gained access to Twitter's internal systems and compromised high-profile accounts including Barack Obama, Elon Musk, and Bill Gates.

How It Happened:

  • Attackers used phone spear phishing ("vishing") to trick employees
  • Gained credentials through a fake internal VPN portal
  • Accessed admin tools to take over accounts

Key Lesson:

Even tech-savvy organizations can fall victim to social engineering. Always verify requests for credentials, especially over phone calls.

The Ubiquiti $46M Fraud (2015)

Attackers impersonated executives to trick finance staff into transferring millions to offshore accounts.

Attack Details:

  • Used spoofed emails appearing to come from executives
  • Created fake documents with company letterhead
  • Exploited time zone differences to prevent verification

Key Lesson:

Financial requests must always be verified through multiple channels, especially for large transactions.

The RSA SecurID Breach (2011)

Attackers compromised RSA's security systems through a simple phishing email with the subject "2011 Recruitment Plan".

Attack Timeline:

  • Targeted low-level employees with realistic-looking Excel attachment
  • Exploited a zero-day vulnerability in Flash
  • Gained access to SecurID token information

Key Lesson:

Even security companies aren't immune. All employees must be vigilant about unexpected attachments.

You receive an email from "HR@yourcompany.com" with the subject "Urgent: Your Account Will Be Locked". It contains a link to "verify your credentials". What should you do?
Click the link and enter your credentials to prevent account lockout
Forward the email to IT security and delete it without clicking
Reply to the email asking if it's legitimate
Incorrect: Never enter credentials from an email link. This is a classic phishing attempt.
Correct! Always report suspicious emails to IT and never interact with questionable links.
Risky: Replying verifies your email is active, which helps attackers target you further.
