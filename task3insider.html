Common Warning Signs

+

Insider threats often exhibit detectable patterns before causing harm. Here are key behaviors to watch for:

Unusual Access Patterns

Accessing systems at odd hours or downloading large amounts of data not needed for their role.

Policy Violations

Repeatedly bypassing security protocols or using unauthorized devices/software.

Behavioral Changes

Sudden mood swings, increased secrecy, or expressing dissatisfaction with the company.

Financial Stress

Unexplained wealth or conversely, visible financial difficulties that might motivate theft.

Unauthorized Devices

Using USB drives or personal cloud storage for work documents without approval.

Job Dissatisfaction

Publicly complaining about the company or managers, especially when combined with other signs.

Which of these would be the MOST concerning combination of behaviors?
An employee who occasionally works late and listens to music at their desk
A worker who complains about their salary during performance reviews
An IT staffer downloading customer databases after being denied a promotion
A manager who uses personal email for some work communication
Correct! The combination of privileged access, negative work event, and suspicious data activity represents the highest risk scenario.
Not quite. While some of these behaviors might be concerning alone, the IT staffer scenario shows multiple high-risk factors combined.

Scenario: The Disgruntled Developer

+

Alex, a senior developer, has been with the company for 5 years. Recently:

  • Was passed over for a promotion he expected
  • Has been making negative comments about management in team chats
  • Started accessing source code repositories he hasn't worked with before
  • Downloaded the entire customer database "for testing purposes"
What should you do in this situation?
Ignore it - senior employees deserve some leeway
Report to security with specific observations and timestamps
Confront Alex directly about his behavior
Wait to see if the behavior continues for another week
Correct! Documenting and reporting concerning behaviors allows security professionals to properly investigate while protecting both the company and employees.
Not the best choice. Insider threats require professional handling - confronting the employee could escalate the situation, while ignoring risks significant damage.

Scenario: The Financial Analyst

+

Jamie in Accounting has:

  • Recently started working late nights and weekends without explanation
  • Been seen taking photos of her screen with her phone
  • Accessed the financial system from a foreign IP address during vacation
  • Suddenly paid off significant personal debt
Which red flags are MOST concerning here?
Working late and the foreign login could have innocent explanations
The combination of screen photos, foreign access, and sudden wealth
Only the screen photos are truly concerning
The paid-off debt is a personal matter, not a security issue
Exactly right. While each item alone might be explainable, the combination suggests potential financial data theft or fraud.
Not quite. Insider threat detection requires looking at patterns of behavior, not just individual actions in isolation.
