TASK 2: TYPES OF SENSITIVE DATA

Not all data requires the same level of protection. Sensitive data refers to information that, if exposed, could result in harm to individuals or organizations. Below are the primary categories of sensitive data you may encounter:

1. Personal Data

Information that can identify an individual either directly or when combined with other data.

Examples: Full name, home address, phone number, email, SSN

2. Financial Data

Information related to financial accounts, transactions, or status that could enable fraud.

Examples: Bank account numbers, credit card details, tax records

3. Health Data

Information about an individual's physical or mental health status and medical history.

Examples: Medical records, insurance information, prescriptions

4. Authentication Data

Credentials and security information used to verify identity and grant access.

Examples: Passwords, PINs, security questions, biometric data

5. Intellectual Property

Proprietary information that gives an organization competitive advantage.

Examples: Trade secrets, patents, source code, business strategies

6. Operational Data

Internal information critical to business operations and continuity.

Examples: Employee records, system configurations, audit logs

Handling Sensitive Data:

  • Always verify who is requesting sensitive data and why
  • Store only what is necessary and delete when no longer needed
  • Use encryption for storage and transmission
  • Follow your organization's data classification policies
  • Report any suspicious requests for sensitive information
  • Never share sensitive data via unsecured channels (e.g., regular email)
