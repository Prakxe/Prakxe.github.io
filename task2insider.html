TYPES OF INSIDER THREATS

Understanding the Threat Landscape

Insider threats come in different forms, each with unique characteristics and risks. Recognizing these types helps in early detection and prevention.

Careless Employees

Well-meaning but negligent employees who bypass security protocols or make mistakes that compromise security.

Real Example:

An employee emails sensitive customer data to their personal account to work from home, accidentally sending it to the wrong address.

Risk Factors: Lack of training, time pressure, convenience over security

Malicious Insiders

Employees who intentionally steal data, sabotage systems, or otherwise harm the organization.

Real Example:

A disgruntled IT administrator deletes critical databases before resigning after being passed over for promotion.

Risk Factors: Financial problems, workplace conflicts, pending termination

Exploited Insiders

Employees whose credentials or access privileges are compromised by external attackers.

Real Example:

An attacker gains an employee's credentials through phishing and uses them to access financial systems.

Risk Factors: Weak passwords, lack of multi-factor authentication, falling for social engineering

Third-Party Threats

Contractors, vendors, or partners with access who misuse their privileges.

Real Example:

A cleaning crew member copies confidential documents left on desks after hours.

Risk Factors: Inadequate vetting, excessive access privileges, lack of monitoring
