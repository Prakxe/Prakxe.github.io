COMMON ATTACK METHODS

The Attacker's Toolkit

Social engineers use these proven methods to bypass security. Being able to identify them is your first line of defense.

Phishing

Fraudulent emails/messages pretending to be from trusted sources to steal credentials or install malware.

Real Example:

"Urgent: Your Microsoft 365 account will be suspended in 24 hours! Click here to verify your identity."

Red Flags: Generic greetings, mismatched sender addresses, urgent language

Pretexting

Creating a fabricated scenario to establish trust and extract information.

Real Example:

"Hi, I'm from IT Support. We're seeing unusual activity on your workstation and need to verify your employee ID and password to investigate."

Red Flags: Requests for sensitive info, pressure to bypass normal procedures

Baiting

Offering something enticing (free software, gift cards) to deliver malware.

Real Example:

USB drives labeled "Q2 Salary Adjustments" left in the office parking lot.

Red Flags: Too-good-to-be-true offers, unexpected physical media

Tailgating/Piggybacking

Gaining physical access by following authorized personnel through secured doors.

Real Example:

"Could you hold the door? I forgot my badge at my desk." (while carrying boxes to appear legitimate)

Red Flags: Requests to bypass security protocols, unfamiliar faces in secure areas
