TASK 4: PROTECTION BEST PRACTICES

You don't need to be a tech expert to protect sensitive data. These simple practices will help you keep information safe in your daily work:

1 Strong Passwords
Create passwords like sentences: "MyDogMaxLoves2Walk!" is stronger than "P@ssw0rd". Change them every 3 months.
2 Secure Sharing
Only share sensitive files through approved company systems - never via personal email or cloud storage.
3 Device Safety
Lock your computer (Windows+L / Command+Control+Q) whenever you step away, even for a minute.
4 Email Caution
Check sender addresses carefully. If an email asks for data or seems odd, verify by phone before responding.

Password Strength Examples:

Weak password:

password123

Good password:

Summer2023!

Strong password:

BlueCoffeeMug@7am

Everyday Situations:

You receive an email from "HR" asking for your employee ID and date of birth
The email looks official but has spelling mistakes and the sender's address is hr.department@randommail.com instead of your company's usual HR email.
What to do:

Don't reply. Forward the email to IT security and call HR directly to verify if they need this information.

A colleague asks you to share a client list via WhatsApp
They say they need it urgently for a meeting, but company policy requires using secure channels for client data.
What to do:

Politely decline and offer to share through the approved company system instead.

A caller claims to be from IT support and asks for your password
They say they need it to fix a problem with your account, but real IT staff will never ask for your password.
What to do:

Hang up immediately. Call IT support directly using the number from the company directory to report the attempt.

Quick Reference Do's and Don'ts:

DO:

  • Use company-approved tools for sharing files
  • Report suspicious messages to IT immediately
  • Lock devices when unattended
  • Verify unusual requests by phone

DON'T:

  • Write passwords on sticky notes
  • Use the same password for multiple accounts
  • Open attachments from unknown senders
  • Plug in unknown USB devices
